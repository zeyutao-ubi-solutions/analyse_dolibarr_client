## Key open questions and source quality

1. **Compliance**: the live `robots.txt` rules could not be obtained, and there is no proof that the underlying HTTP requests were spaced at least 1 second apart. This recovered research draft must not be described as a completed, compliant full crawl.
2. **Coverage**: the previous report mentioned five languages (French, English, Spanish, German, Italian); this list can only confirm the 40 main-domain pages kept by the search/page index. Possibly missing language versions of articles, pagination and blog posts need an additional check.
3. **Name roles**: the exact roles of names such as Fenotag, 3A SOLUTIONS SAS and SNCF (customer, integration partner or technology partner) need business verification. Brother is an equipment partner explicitly labelled on the official website, not a target end customer; see its brand product page.
4. **Segmentation conflicts**: a hierarchy/multi-label policy needs to be decided for the three retail segments, the three logistics segments, the four linen-chain segments, and general manufacturing vs. automotive/aerospace.
5. **Soundness of inferences**: the four segments TGL (textile manufacturing), Eaux de Zilia (beverage bottling), Orange (telecom) and Banque de France (finance) come from customer cases, but whether these industries are broad enough still needs commercial validation.
6. **Scale**: where there is no explicit evidence on the official website, everything is recorded as "None"; employee counts must not be used on our own initiative as a hard threshold. The version of the LinkedIn standard industries also needs verification.

## Classification rules

First identify the business the company **operates**, then compute positive and exclusion signals within the matching segment. The mere presence of technology words such as `RFID`, `IoT`, `BLE`, `WMS`, `cloud` or `asset tracking` is not enough to identify a company as a UBI prospect end user. Separate exclusion/manual-review rules should be set for integrators, hardware makers, consultancies, distributors and competitors.
